The Jam

GitHub's Game Off is a month-long jam: a theme drops on November 1, a game ships by December 1, and the whole thing lives in a public repository. The 2020 theme was Moonshot. Our three-person team, Ascended Games, read it two ways at once: the moonshot thinking of a mouse deciding to get itself to the moon, and the literal launch of a rocket at it.

The premise: after months as a lab mouse, the experiments have had one wonderful side effect, enhanced intelligence. Every night you stare out of your cage at the big ball of cheese in the sky. Then you notice the tubes the lab keeps firing at it, and decide to sneak onto one.

The Game

A 2D pixel-art stealth platformer: a side-scrolling lab full of scientists to avoid, objects to hide behind, and a rocket at the end. Non-violent, single-player, a few minutes long, with subtitles and a speedrun tag for the people who found the hiding-spot puzzles too easy. Built in Unity with Aseprite and GIMP for the art, released for Windows, macOS, and Linux on itch.io, with a v1.0.1 patch two days later so it would install through the itch app.

What I did

Most of the art: the laboratory tilesets and backgrounds, the props you hide behind, the rocket, and the title screen, drawn in Aseprite and GIMP. The mouse, the scientists, and the end-credits image were a teammate's. And all of the music: I picked royalty-free tracks for the intro, the level, the ending, and the credits, then cut each one into a seamless loop in Reaper so nothing in the lab ever audibly restarts.

Three of us built it in the month, working in one repository the way the jam intends: 351 commits in the jam month, and 120 of those were mine (across two accounts; I locked myself out of the first one mid-jam and made a second). The code is GPL-3.0 and the assets are CC BY 4.0, so anyone can pull the repo apart and see how it's put together.

Results

The main complaint from the people who rated it was that it was too short and they wanted more of it. For a game built in thirty days by three people, that's the complaint you want: nobody said the mouse felt bad to move, or that the music got old, or that they couldn't tell what to do. They finished it and looked for the next level.
