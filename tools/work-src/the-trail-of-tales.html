The Idea

The Trail of Tales started as a YouTube channel. The format was a book summary told from inside the book: a video narrated from the perspective of a major character, with a script written to set up the world without spoiling it, voiced to match the character's personality. The goal was to get people reading fantasy and sci-fi series that deserved more attention than they were getting.

In April 2024 I built the written half. Selling Streamershaven had left a gap; this was the subject I was already spending every evening on, and the site gave the channel a home for the reviews, the catalogs, and the shop.

The Content

Reviews with a number on them

Book reviews scored out of five and labeled in the title, from a 4.4 for Qing's Quest to a 1.8 for Scarlet Citadel, with the reasoning shown and a disclosure up front when I didn't finish the book. A review site that only publishes praise isn't a review site.

Author catalogs

Complete, dated reading-order catalogs for authors in the LitRPG and progression-fantasy space (Will Wight, Sarah Lin, BT Narro, Travis Starnes, James Ghoul, A.F. Kay), the pages people search for when they finish one series and want the next.

Video summaries

The YouTube side, embedded on the site: character-perspective summaries with unique scripts and voice acting, promoted with the three-point pitch on the homepage: character perspective, unique scripts, smooth narration.

Initiate Auramancy

A LitRPG novel I'd been working on for years, serialized on the site as a web novel: Eithan Rogers, a young man targeted by assassins after his mentor does something unforgivable, in a world called Gaun where magic is rare, distrusted, and called Aura Manipulation. Publishing chapters publicly was the way to make myself finish it, with the Royal Road model in mind: keep the early chapters free, publish the book when it's done. It came with a world guide and a commissioned cover.

The Shop

A WooCommerce store built around the reader: aluminum bookmarks with page-holding tabs, seasonal designs, print-it-yourself bookmark downloads at $2.99–$3.99, and print-on-demand mugs, shirts, and phone cases for the genre ("LitRPG: the fantasy book genre for gamers"). Books were listed with Amazon affiliate links rather than stocked. A Discord community and an article-request form rounded it out.

Results

The Trail of Tales is the most recent of the sites I've run for myself, and the one that pointed most directly at what came next: the reviews and the web novel are the reading and writing habits that turned into the novel I'm finishing now.
