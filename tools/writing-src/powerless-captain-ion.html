Clu-clunk.

He shut the door and locked it, standing now on the moonlit street. The biting cold nipped at Marcus’s exposed nose and forehead despite his preparations. Kneeling down to ensure his boots were tightly bound to his feet, he clapped his cheeks with his glove-wrapped hands.

“Right then...” he said, energy bubbling. He rounded the corner of Foster Road and Westminster Street, where he saw some children playing in the park, their labored breaths frosting in the chilly October night. One of the boys wore a Captain Ion costume, a bright red cape and shiny blue tights. Yellow letters with a crimson outline lay in the center of the design: CI. Red streaks symbolizing his power ran in spokes around it. He smiled, thinking of the comic books sitting buried in his study. He waved to them as he said, “Good evening!”

The other boy, armored in dull purple carapace plates surrounded by a dense mist, stopped mid-charge. He waved awkwardly, as if he were embarrassed, and the boy wearing the costume threw a Captain Ion salute. Marcus returned the salute, grinning. He noticed a few of the plates on the armored boy were glowing blue and flaking away like burnt paper, and the eyes of the boy in the costume glowed a dull red.

Must be playing the arch-nemesis role… Marcus guessed.

“Be careful, Flammanator. Captain Ion is a tough opponent.” He winked, and the boy in the purple armor lit up like a Christmas tree. “How’d you know?”

“Are you kidding? The Captain Ion comics are one of my favorites! Who else would you be?”

“Yeah… yeah! I’m The Flammanator! Hear me roar!” He roared for emphasis.

Vanessa pouted. “What about me?” she asked indignantly.

Marcus smiled, raised a hand to his mouth in mock shock, and replied, “Why, the Wicked Witch of the Wind could never be bested by the likes of Captain Ion, right?” He winked to the boys, who got in on the fun. “Yeah, you’re strong, Vanessa. We couldn’t beat you even if we worked together, right, Johnny?!” The armored boy nodded with a smile.

Her eyes glistened through her orange goggles, and she smiled gleefully. “And don’t you forget it!” she said, then, as if to punctuate her statement, she weaved her pattern and released a powerful gust of air that blew the boys onto their behinds. Her face strained a shade of blue from the working, which quickly returned to normal a moment later.

She then bowed to Marcus gracefully, like a princess, then helped the boys up. “It’s getting late, we should head home. Good night, mister,” she said sagely, and they took off running towards wherever they lived.

Haven’t seen those boys before. Vanessa must be making new friends at school. Marcus thought to himself. He smiled. Good for her!

Marcus felt even more energized after talking to the children, and with renewed vigor he began his jog in earnest.
