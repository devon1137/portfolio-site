Footsteps. Pacing now. Marcus kept his eyes closed and tracked the sound. Ian was moving in a circle around the group.

Jasmine

“So. My turn to share, I guess.” Ian’s voice had taken on a different quality now. Less manic. More... hollow. Like some of her more severe cases. “My name is Ian Boraghast. I’m twenty-seven years old. I’ve been invisible for three years, two months, and fourteen days. Not that I’m counting or anything.”

The footsteps stopped. Only the low hum of the space heater could be heard in the deafening silence.

“My Navigator power is called ‘Perceptual Editing.’” He finally said, then paused again, as if waiting for a reaction from people. When it didn’t come, he let out a resigned sigh. “Sounds fancy, right? What it ACTUALLY means is that your brain—everyone’s brain, really—actively edits me out. Almost like a mask in Photoshop, but for consciousness. I exist in the negative space between what you see and what you don’t see.”

A pause. Jasmine heard someone—Nathan, maybe—shift uncomfortably.

“Used to be, I could turn it on and off. Pop in, pop out.” A manic cackle followed. “Useful for avoiding my sperm donor’s fists. Useful for escaping bullies. Useful for existing without being perceived—which, let me tell you, is AMAZING when you’re a kid getting the shit kicked out of you.”

The footsteps stopped. Right beside Jasmine. Marcus squeezed her hand twice. Reassuring. It’s okay. It didn’t help. Sweat beaded at her forehead.

“But then the Sundering happened. And now?” Ian’s voice was directly in her ear, barely a whisper. “It’s always on. Can’t turn it off. Haven’t been SEEN in three years. But did they search for me? Oh no. No missing person flyers. No search parties led by bloodhounds. Nothing.”

Jasmine jerked away, eyes flying open. She looked right where the voice had come from.

Nothing. Empty space.

“You’re looking right at me,” Ian whispered. “I’m standing six inches from your face. I can see the fear in your eyes. I can count your eyelashes. But you?” A pause. “Your brain just... slides right past me. Like I’m not even here.”

“Jesus Christ,” Jasmine breathed.

Ian cackled. “Not quite. Ian Bor-a-ghast. Common mistake.” The voice moved away. “Oh, and Marcus? Fun fact: I was there. On Carmichael Street. Walking right beside you two the entire way here.”

Jasmine’s hand went rigid in Marcus’s grip. “You were FOLLOWING us?” Her voice had that professional edge—Dream Tender mode activating.

“Following is such a harsh word,” Ian said, sounding genuinely hurt. “I was... accompanying? I mean, how else was I supposed to get here? Can’t exactly drive—who’d see me coming? My power extends to things I touch, so cars are out. And walking alone in the RFZ when you’re imperceptible is... lonely. Dangerous. You two were heading this way anyway, so...”

“You could have SAID something,” Jasmine said, her voice tight.

“Could I?” Ian asked quietly. The manic energy had dropped away, leaving something raw underneath. “Would you have believed me? A disembodied voice in the dark, in the RFZ, saying, ‘Hey, don’t mind me, just walking beside you?’ You would’ve thought you were hallucinating. You would’ve been MORE scared. Sure, Marcus could have said, ‘Oh, it’s just that Ian guy. I owe him ten buckeroos for a manga, quick, run for it!’”

A pause. Then a sigh. “I deflect. I run. I hide. But I don’t incite fear in family.” He paused. “Even shit-for-brains brothers who run off for two years on a fruitless quest to discover the origin of his shitshow situation.” He sighed. A tone of sincerity followed. “Not when they are already terrified shitless. The RFZ? That’s no joking matter. It’s serious shit.”

A tap-tap-tap, flesh against metal. His fingers against the chair?

“I was trying to be considerate,” Ian said softly. “Which is... a rarity for me nowadays. So. Sorry, I really don’t stalk people. We just happened to be going the same direction. I kept quiet because I didn’t want to terrify you more than you already were. Jasmine, you were practically vibrating with fear. Marcus, you kept trying to do that thing with your hands—”

“The conjuring motion,” Marcus said numbly.

“Yeah, that. Thirty-four times since I started following you, by the way. Thirty-four times you tried to summon light that wouldn’t come. I counted.”

Marcus stiffened in her hand.

“AND WITH THAT OUT OF THE WAY,” Ian’s voice suddenly cranked up to manic eleven cheerfulness, “I’m back, bitches! Did you miss me? Don’t answer that. Oh, look, Nathan’s shaking his head. I can see you, you know.”

He laughed. “Invisible man joke.”
