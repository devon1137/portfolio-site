Notes of Yore

A Dungeons & Dragons site that started as a storefront for session notebooks I'd designed and turned into a content site: a complete guide to every 5e condition, homebrew rules, a WooCommerce shop, and a worldbuilding fiction series.

The Idea

I'd started playing D&D during the pandemic with a group that became some of my closest friends, and I'd designed a session notebook for us because we kept losing track of who we'd met and what we'd promised to do. Notes of Yore began in September 2021 as a small publisher's storefront for that notebook. Building the store, I realized I had more to say about the game than I had products to sell, and the site became a content site with a shop attached.

After Streamershaven, which was technical by nature, I wanted a subject with more creative room. This one had it: rules, homebrew, and eventually fiction.

The Writing

The backbone is a complete guide to the fifteen conditions in 5th Edition: Blinded, Charmed, Deafened, Exhaustion, Frightened, Grappled, Incapacitated, Invisible, Paralyzed, Petrified, Poisoned, Prone, Restrained, Stunned, and Unconscious. Each article covers what the condition does, what causes it (creatures, spells, terrain, traps), how to counter it, and how bad it really is, written for players and game masters both, with a homebrew rule or two where the official ones felt thin.

Around that: homebrew metamagic and race-specific feats, house rules to speed up combat, a virtual-tabletop comparison, session-zero guidance, and the small practical questions people actually search ("how do you roll a d100?"). Each post got a table of contents, a featured image, and structured on-page SEO, the same discipline as Streamershaven applied to a subject I was still learning. One of the homebrew pieces, 5E Alternate Combat Rules to Speed up Combat, is reproduced in full as a writing sample.

Cartographer's Notebook

A worldbuilding fiction series for game masters, published as Google Web Stories: short pieces like The Giant's Hill, The Croaking Pond, and Varian Fort Ruins, each a location a GM could drop into a campaign. It's the earliest public version of the fiction writing that became the novel I'm finishing now.

The Products

The shop ran on WooCommerce and sold things I'd made. The Adventure Log is a 7×10 paperback session notebook, 120 pages, 40 of them a templated layout I designed around what our table kept forgetting: a session header for hit points, spell slots, and coin; boxes for places of interest, who you met, quests accepted, noteworthy events, and loot; and a small tracker for rope, ball bearings, pitons, and rations, with a lined page facing each one (the spread is in Screens below). Alongside it, printable notebook templates (an Adventure Log and a Sorcerer Log at $2.99), and a marbled blue polyhedral dice set. The cover illustration is by Barbara Amaral, who I commissioned for it; the layout and the templates were mine. It was published independently in August 2021, a month before the site, in paperback and hardcover.

The site is gone, but the notebook is still in print: the Adventure Log on Amazon.

Results

Notes of Yore was a shorter run than Streamershaven and I never scaled it the same way; it was the site where I found out I wanted to write fiction. The conditions series is still the most complete thing I've written about a game.
