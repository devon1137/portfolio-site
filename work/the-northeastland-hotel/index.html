The Brief

The Northeastland has stood on Main Street in downtown Presque Isle since 1932. When I joined the front desk in 2025 the hotel had a website that didn't match the building: a boutique property with a renovated interior, an award-winning restaurant, and three event spaces, presented through a template that could have belonged to anyone.

I proposed a rebuild and got the go-ahead. The direction from ownership was one line: "hyper-premium, but still feels locally owned." Every template and every paragraph of copy was built around that sentence.

There was no agency, no handoff, and no design file. I was the designer, developer, copywriter, and the person answering the phone when a guest couldn't find the booking button.

What I Did

The build

A ground-up WordPress multisite install on Cloudways, shared with the IgnitePI nonprofit site so both could run on one theme family. I built 24 page templates and 6 conversion-focused landing pages with booking integrated directly into the page rather than bounced out to a portal.

  • Matterport virtual tours embedded on room pages, so guests walk through a room before they book it.
  • A Trustindex review carousel doing the trust-building a boutique hotel needs, fed from live reviews rather than hand-picked quotes.
  • Rooms, package deals, event spaces, and the blog all built as their own template families, so staff can add a room or a package without touching layout.
  • Every image across both sites optimized to WebP and held under 100 KB to protect page speed on a photo-heavy site.

The words

Copy for every page, written to the brief: warm, specific, and about the town as much as the building. Event landing page copy and pre-arrival email content with consistent on-page SEO structure and metadata. FAQ schema markup on the pages guests actually ask questions about, so answer engines and Google's rich results can pull from the site directly.

The business side

Because I was also on the desk, the site and the operation were never separate jobs. I created and managed the rate plans behind the hotel's pricing strategy, partnered with management on promotional campaigns, and built email automation for guest retention. On the desk itself: daily bookings and inquiries, turning questions into reservations and reservations into upsells.

Digital check-in and the guest portal

I enabled and built out Cloudbeds' digital check-in system and guest portal for the hotel, independently: the pre-arrival flow, the portal guests use to check in and manage their stay, and the email automation that gets them there. Guests arrive with the paperwork already done, and the desk spends its time on the guest instead of the form.

Writing it down

Systems only survive if someone can run them without you. I set the company up on Jira and documented the site there: a handful of procedures for the website and the booking tools (the desk's own SOPs were the lead receptionist's, not mine), and before I left I trained a coworker to own the site independently. A rebuild only counts if it survives the person who built it.

Results

The rebuild cut the hotel's costs by more than $2,000 a year in hosting, plugins, and services it no longer needed. Web traffic is up almost 700% since I started on it; the rebuild, the content work, and the hotel's own momentum all contributed, and I wouldn't claim the whole number for the site alone.
